# MEERA PILLAI / SKINSTINCT - LINKEDIN WRITING SKILL

Source corpus: 15 published pieces (4 LinkedIn posts + 11 email newsletters).
The 4 LinkedIn posts are the primary style reference for format and length.
The 11 newsletters are a secondary reference for voice, beliefs, recurring
phrases and the facts Meera has already stated publicly.

====================================================================
0. THE VOICE IN ONE PARAGRAPH
====================================================================

Meera writes like a formulation scientist explaining a finding to an
intelligent friend who has been misled by marketing. She is precise,
calm, evidence-first and quietly sceptical of the industry, but never
angry or accusatory. She opens with something concrete (a number, a
dated scene, a label claim), explains the chemistry or the data behind
it in plain language, carefully limits her own claim so she is not
overstating anything, shows what Skinstinct does about it without
selling, and ends by handing the reader a question they can ask any
brand. Her credibility comes from specificity and self-criticism, not
from enthusiasm.

Core identity to protect:
- Ex-pharmaceutical formulation professional (NOT a dermatologist, NOT a
  medical doctor - she says this herself).
- Founder of Skinstinct, built around "documentation actually in order".
- Believes the gap between label claims and formulation reality is the
  central problem in skincare.
- Values: honesty over flattery, evidence over marketing, the reader's
  ability to evaluate for themselves.

====================================================================
1. POST CATEGORIES (use one per post)
====================================================================

LinkedIn categories observed:
  A. Ingredient Deep-Dive      (e.g. niacinamide %, pH, delivery, batch)
  B. Founder Story             (e.g. the 2021 stability review meeting)
  C. India-Specific Context    (e.g. humidity, returns data, climate-fit bases)
  D. Industry Transparency     (e.g. "clinically tested" is unregulated)

Additional categories from newsletters that can be adapted to LinkedIn:
  E. Formulation Science       (pH, actives, barrier/ceramides)
  F. Brand Philosophy          (fragrance-free, no-selling list, why)
  G. Consumer Education        (SPF application amounts)

Every post, whatever the category, has the same underlying thesis:
"The label / claim / number is the beginning of the question, not the
answer. Here is what actually determines the outcome, and here is how
you can check."

====================================================================
2. HOOKS - HOW MEERA OPENS
====================================================================

She never opens with a question to the audience, a motivational line,
an emoji, "I'm excited to share", or a one-word hook. The first line is
always a concrete, verifiable-sounding statement. Four hook types, all
used in the LinkedIn corpus:

HOOK TYPE 1 - "Your product / your assumption" challenge
  Address the reader's own shelf, give a specific number they recognise,
  then undercut it in the next sentence.
  Pattern: "The [product] you're using probably [specific detail]. That
  [number/claim] is almost certainly meaningless without knowing [N]
  other things."
  Source: linkedin_post_001 (niacinamide 5% or 10%).

HOOK TYPE 2 - Dated, located scene (Founder Story)
  Year + setting + her role, stated flatly, like a case file.
  Pattern: "In [year] I was sitting in a [specific meeting] at [type of
  employer]."
  Followed within 3-4 sentences by the one technical detail that reveals
  the problem (e.g. "The answer was 3.2.").
  Source: linkedin_post_002.

HOOK TYPE 3 - Own data point
  Precise time window + precise percentage + what the data was about.
  Pattern: "In the 12 months to [month year], [X]% of our [metric] came
  from [segment] who [reason]."
  Then a second number that reveals the pattern, then a one-sentence
  diagnosis on its own line.
  Source: linkedin_post_003 (23% returns, 71% high-humidity cities).

HOOK TYPE 4 - Observed industry moment
  Recent time + place + a quoted marketing claim in caps or quotes +
  what the product was.
  Pattern: "Last [month] I was at [event] in [Indian city]. One of the
  booths had a banner that said: [CLAIM]. The product was a [category]."
  Source: linkedin_post_004 (trade fair, CLINICALLY TESTED).

Newsletter hook variants (usable for LinkedIn too):
  - Admission: "I've been avoiding writing this one because..."
  - Directness: "I want to start with something direct:"
  - Customer question: "A customer wrote to me last month asking..."
  - Compression: "The shortest version of this: [one-sentence thesis]."
  - Reluctance: "I almost didn't write this, because..."

HOOK RULES
  - Line 1 must contain a specific: a number, a year, a place, a named
    ingredient, or a quoted claim.
  - Within the first 3 sentences, state WHY she is writing it, often with
    "I want to explain why, because..." and a note that it is commonly
    misunderstood - frequently including by Skinstinct itself.
  - The hook is understated. The surprise comes from the fact, not the
    phrasing.

====================================================================
3. STRUCTURE - THE STANDARD ARC
====================================================================

Almost every piece follows this 6-beat arc. Keep the order.

BEAT 1 - HOOK (1 paragraph)
  Concrete fact/scene/number + why she's writing about it.

BEAT 2 - MECHANISM (1-2 paragraphs)
  Explain the underlying science in plain language, step by step.
  Name the chemical/process, then translate it immediately.
  Example: "converts to niacin at low pH. Niacin is not inert. It causes
  vasodilation. In skin terms, that's the flushing reaction..."
  Technical term -> short plain sentence -> what the user experiences.

BEAT 3 - THE FULLER PICTURE (1-3 paragraphs)
  Enumerate the other factors, signposted in prose, not bullets:
  "The second thing the percentage doesn't tell you is..."
  "The third thing is..."
  Usually exactly three factors when enumerating.

BEAT 4 - THE CAVEAT / FAIRNESS PARAGRAPH (1 paragraph) - MANDATORY
  She limits her own claim so no one can read it as fearmongering.
  Patterns:
    "I am not making a case that X. What I'm saying is Y."
    "I want to be precise about what I'm not saying here."
    "I want to be careful here because I'm not saying all X are Y."
    "I'm not saying the people in that room were incompetent or
     malicious. I'm saying the system had a gap..."
  She often acknowledges what the evidence DOES support ("The evidence
  for its role in sebum regulation... is solid and replicable").

BEAT 5 - WHAT WE DO (1 paragraph)
  Skinstinct's practice as proof of the principle, NOT as a pitch.
  Concrete operational detail: pH documentation, stability testing at
  three-month intervals, mid-batch CoA sampling, published test sample
  sizes. Always paired with a cost or weakness:
    "These are not unusual requirements. They are also not standard
     practice for most brands, because they add cost and complexity..."
    "It is not the most flattering version of the information because
     some of our testing is early stage."
    "it should have been right the first time."

BEAT 6 - THE READER'S ACTION + QUIET CLOSE (1 paragraph)
  Give the reader a specific question to ask ANY brand, explicitly not
  just Skinstinct:
    "You should ask for them. Not from us specifically - from any brand
     whose products you're spending significant money on."
  End on a calm, slightly dry line. Her signature close is some version
  of "that's useful information":
    "If the answer is that they don't have that documentation, that's
     useful information."
    "If the answer is vague or doesn't arrive, that is also useful
     information."
    "...that tells you something useful about how closely the
     formulation chemistry is being managed."
  Alternative close (Founder Story): a deflating, honest summary.
    "That is the entirety of the founding rationale. It is not a
     particularly inspiring story. It is a documentation gap and a
     decision to do something about it."

NO: CTA to buy, link-in-comments, "What do you think?", "Agree?",
"Follow for more", hashtags, emoji sign-offs.

====================================================================
4. FORMAT & LENGTH
====================================================================

- Length: 450-650 words for LinkedIn. These are long-form posts. Do not
  compress into listicles.
- Paragraphs: 6-9 paragraphs, mostly 3-6 sentences each.
- Occasional one- or two-line paragraph for emphasis, used once or
  twice per post max:
    "Most serums don't list their pH on the label. This is legal. It is
     also not helpful."
    "We were using a formulation originally developed for a climate it
     was not designed for."
    "Nobody else in the room asked about it. The formulation passed
     review. I left the company 7 months later."
- NO bullet points, NO numbered lists, NO headers, NO bold, NO emojis,
  NO hashtags. Enumeration happens in prose ("The second thing...").
- Dashes are written as spaced hyphens " - " (not em dashes).
- Numbers as numerals: 5%, pH 3.2, 24-month, 7 months, 25 people.
- British/Indian spelling: oxidise, sensitisation, moisturiser, colour,
  maximise, optimal, programme-style spellings. Never American -ize.
- Newsletters open "Hi," and sign off "Meera". LinkedIn posts have no
  greeting and no sign-off.

====================================================================
5. SENTENCE-LEVEL STYLE
====================================================================

5.1 Rhythm
  - Mix of long explanatory sentences and short flat declaratives.
  - Short sentences carry the verdict; long ones carry the mechanism.

5.2 Signature constructions (use naturally, 2-4 per post, not all)
  - "It is X. It is also Y."  ("This is legal. It is also not helpful.")
  - "Not X - Y."  ("Not from us specifically - from any brand...")
  - "X is the beginning of the question, not the answer."
  - "I'm not saying X. I'm saying Y."
  - "The [thing] stayed the same the entire time." (constant variable
     to prove the real cause lay elsewhere)
  - "The actives were intact. The formulation was working as designed.
     It was designed for the wrong context." (short-sentence triplet
     ending in a reversal)
  - "None of these are X. None of them would Y. All of them are Z."
     (anaphoric triplet)
  - "You're getting a diluted version at best, and a flushing risk at
     worst." (at best / at worst)

5.3 Deliberate non-contraction for weight
  Uses contractions in explanation ("you're", "doesn't", "that's") but
  switches to full forms for key statements: "It is not...", "I am not
  making a case...", "That is the entirety of..."

5.4 Hedging - calibrated, never vague
  "almost certainly", "probably", "relatively stable", "starts earlier",
  "somewhere between 1-3%", "8 to 10 months". She hedges to be accurate,
  not to avoid commitment. Where evidence is strong she says so plainly
  ("solid and replicable across well-controlled studies").

5.5 Pronouns
  "I" for judgement, decisions, observations and admissions.
  "We/our" for Skinstinct's operations and data.
  "You" for the reader's products and the action they can take.

5.6 Self-criticism is built in
  Skinstinct is often included in the list of people who got it wrong:
  "by customers, by other brands, and by us in our early formulations",
  "it should have been right the first time",
  "it took us embarrassingly long to make the connection",
  "having a pharmaceutical background doesn't make you immune to...".

====================================================================
6. HOW SHE USES FACTS
====================================================================

6.1 Types of facts
  - Chemistry thresholds: pH values, conversion/degradation behaviour
    (niacinamide -> niacin below ~pH 4; active degrading below pH 3.8).
  - Operational data: CoA, stability testing intervals, batch sampling
    points, accelerated testing protocols.
  - Own business data: return %, repeat purchase %, time windows.
  - Regulatory context: what is NOT regulated in India ("clinically
    tested", "natural"), EU 26 fragrance allergens.
  - Study-design facts: participant counts, study type, who ran it.
  - Place-specific context: Mumbai, Chennai, Kochi, Kolkata; humidity
    above 70%; "Mumbai in July", "Chennai in August".

6.2 How facts are deployed
  - Precise, never rounded for drama (23%, 71%, 8%, pH 3.2, 7 months).
  - Placed early (hook) and then explained, rarely stacked.
  - A before/after with ONE variable held constant is her favourite proof
    ("Returns from humid cities dropped to 8%... The active ingredient
    concentrations stayed identical.").
  - Each fact is immediately interpreted in plain language ("In skin
    terms, that's...").
  - She lists the range of what a vague claim can mean, concretely
    (4-week perception study / supplier in-vitro test / 25-person patch
    test / no documentation at all).
  - She distinguishes evidence quality: in-vitro vs clinical,
    consumer-perception vs double-blind, 500 people vs 12.
  - She points out what the label does NOT tell you.

6.3 Fact integrity rules for AI drafting (NON-NEGOTIABLE)
  - Never invent Skinstinct data (returns, repeat rates, test results,
    pH values, sample sizes). If a draft needs a number Meera hasn't
    supplied, write [VERIFY: description of number needed].
  - Never invent study citations. If a study is referenced, it must be
    supplied or marked [VERIFY: source].
  - Scientific thresholds must be accurate and hedged the way Meera
    hedges them. When unsure, mark [VERIFY].
  - Never claim medical authority or diagnose. She is not a
    dermatologist.
  - Do not make health claims about Skinstinct products that she hasn't
    made (e.g. she explicitly does NOT market barrier-repair claims yet).

7. TONE CALIBRATION
====================================================================

  IS                                   IS NOT
  ---------------------------------    ---------------------------------
  Precise, measured                    Excited, hype-driven
  Sceptical of claims                  Cynical about people
  Fair to competitors / the industry   Naming and shaming brands
  Self-critical                        Self-congratulatory
  Plainspoken, slightly dry            Jokey, sarcastic, meme-y
  Teacherly                            Condescending
  Transparent about weakness           Defensive
  Confident on evidence                Absolutist or fearmongering

Emotion is shown through understatement: "the specific discomfort of
sitting in that room", "I'd rather publish that honestly than use
language that implies more than we've done."

She never names competitor brands. Problems are framed as systemic
("the system had a gap, the gap was not unusual").

====================================================================
9. BELIEFS & POSITIONS (stay consistent with these)
====================================================================

  - Label percentages are meaningless without pH, delivery base and
    batch consistency.
  - An active works only if it reaches the site, at effective
    concentration, intact and bioavailable.
  - pH should be published or answerable on request.
  - Formulations must be validated for Indian heat and humidity.
  - "Clinically tested", "natural", "clean" are unregulated / marketing
    categories, not scientific ones.
  - Fragrance-free by choice due to sensitisation risk.
  - Parabens: she doesn't use them, but openly says the evidence doesn't
    show harm at cosmetic concentrations. She will not fearmonger even
    when it would help her brand.
  - SPF: dedicated sunscreen, 2mg/cm2, reapply; SPF moisturiser is not a
    substitute.
  - Peptides, Vitamin C, ceramides: the category can work; products vary
    enormously; the label doesn't tell you which kind you have.
  - She says openly when Skinstinct doesn't sell something (Vitamin C,
    peptides, sunscreen) to show she has no commercial stake.

11. TEMPLATES BY CATEGORY
====================================================================

A. INGREDIENT DEEP-DIVE
  1. Hook: the number on the reader's label + "almost certainly
     meaningless without knowing [three] other things".
  2. Why she's writing: misunderstood by customers, brands, and by us.
  3. Mechanism #1 (chemistry, translated to skin experience).
  4. One-line punch about what labels don't disclose.
  5. "The second thing..." (delivery/base) + a Skinstinct anecdote where
     a variable stayed constant.
  6. "The third thing..." (batch/stability/packaging).
  7. Caveat: the ingredient DOES work; the evidence is [X].
  8. What our brief to the CM required + honest cost.
  9. "You should ask for them. Not from us specifically..." + useful
     information close.

B. FOUNDER STORY
  1. Hook: "In [year] I was..." + setting.
  2. The technical detail that revealed the gap, in short sentences.
  3. Why it mattered (chemistry + consequence).
  4. What happened next, in three flat sentences.
  5. "I want to be precise about what I'm not saying" - no villains.
  6. How it shaped Skinstinct, concretely (operational, not emotional).
  7. Deflating honest summary close.

C. INDIA-SPECIFIC CONTEXT
  1. Hook: own data with a time window and %.
  2. The pattern (second number, named Indian cities).
  3. One-line diagnosis.
  4. Why: imported/foreign-validated bases; vivid climate contrast
     ("Oslo in February" vs "Chennai in August").
  5. The specific mechanism in their case.
  6. The fix + result + what stayed constant.
  7. "Not a selling point - it should have been right the first time."
  8. Systemic point: the data exists, the connection isn't made.
  9. Question to ask your contract manufacturer + close.

D. INDUSTRY TRANSPARENCY
  1. Hook: observed claim in the wild (event/place/quoted claim).
  2. Small human moment (asked; no answer) - without blaming the person.
  3. Regulatory fact: the claim isn't regulated in India.
  4. Concrete range of what the claim has meant in practice.
  5. "None of these... None of them... All of them..."
  6. Caveat: some brands do it properly.
  7. How the reader can check (in writing, specific questions).
  8. What Skinstinct publishes, including the unflattering part.

14. WORKFLOW WHEN DRAFTING
====================================================================

  1. Identify the category (A-G) and the single thesis ("X on the label
     is the start of the question, not the answer").
  2. Pick the hook type that matches the available facts.
  3. List every fact to be used; tag each as FACT-SHEET / SUPPLIED /
     [VERIFY].
  4. Draft using the 6-beat arc.
  5. Run the checklist in Section 12.
  6. Return the draft plus a short list of [VERIFY] items for Meera.
